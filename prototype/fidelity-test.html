  • Your pick, rebuilt in 3D: B's body kept face-for-face — same 31 body faces, same straightened arches, same outline weight 4 — with C's wheels swapped in: 16-sided discs and hubs, so the tyre is round instead of B's 8-gon.
  • Same 37-face budget as B — only the vertex count rises (226 vs 182), because the far-side wheels are modelled too — a 3/4 view shows four wheel positions, not two. Nothing got more detailed; the shape language changed: round where it buys legibility, faceted where it carries identity.
  • Why it should hold up: at 72 px the silhouette and the greenhouse are what make it your car, while a wheel only has to read as "wheel" — and a round disc reads faster than a faceted one.
  • Trade-off to watch: a round disc inside B's angular arch can read as pasted-on. The disc keeps C's shape at B's outline weight (4), not C's 7, so one line weight still runs across the whole car.
  • Trade-off to watch: this is the first mixed fidelity target, so the pipeline needs a per-region rule (wheels round, body faceted) instead of one global stylisation strength.

Implied pipeline · shape-locking img2img at B's mesh tolerance, plus a per-region guardrail (mask / inpaint) that forces the wheels to stay round. A little more plumbing than one global strength — but the cheapest way to simplify without spending identity.

Session-5 pick recorded: D · Hybrid — B's body with C's wheels. Tap any card to re-record; nothing is saved.

Method: one car, defined once as a low-poly 3D mesh in tools/gen-car-3d.js, projected through a fixed orthographic camera (yaw −36°, pitch 15°) — no perspective, so nothing foreshortens — then back-face culled, depth-sorted and flat-shaded into SVG. Every variant is that same mesh: a Douglas-Peucker simplification of one dense side profile (tolerance 1.1 · 3.1 · 7.4 model units), so no variant was drawn independently and the drift is measured rather than eyeballed. Palette locked as before (cinnabar · gold · azurite · ink · rice paper). Facets = polygons actually rendered after culling, split body + wheel (all four wheel positions — the far pair is visible through the arch openings); the contact shadow and the photo's backdrop/grain are marked non-faces and excluded. So: A 56 (48+8) · B 37 (31+6) · C 19 (13+6) · D 37 (31+6). Profile pts = vertices in the simplified side outline; dev = the worst gap between that outline and the dense one, in model units (the car is 252 long and 122 tall, so dev 2.7 is about a tyre's tread depth).
D stays the Session-5 pick — B's body kept face-for-face with C's wheels swapped in. In mesh terms it spends the same 37 faces as B and only raises the wheel's segment count, which is why B and D read as one car at two wheel resolutions.
